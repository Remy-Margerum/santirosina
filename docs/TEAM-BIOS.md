## Doug Margerum — Director of Winemaking

As received:

> For more than forty years, Doug Margerum has been one of the defining figures of Santa Barbara wine. He fell for wine
> as a child, traveling through France with his parents, and grew up working in restaurants. In 1981, the year he
> graduated from UC Santa Barbara, his family bought the Wine Cask. Over the next 26 years he built it into one of
> California's most respected wine destinations. In 1994 it won Wine Spectator's Grand Award, one of only 74 restaurants
> worldwide to earn it that year. His Wine Cask Futures program, launched in 1984, helped bring Santa Barbara wines to a
> national audience. He was also the first to bring producers like Didier Dagueneau, René Geoffroy and Dominique Laurent
> to the United States.
>
> In 1986, Doug co-founded Vita Nova with Bob Lindquist of Qupé and Jim Clendenen of Au Bon Climat. In 2001 he started
> Margerum Wine Company in a 240-square-foot space behind Brander Vineyard. It has since grown into one of the county's
> most celebrated wineries, alongside his Barden label from the Sta. Rita Hills and his 18-acre Margerum Estate planted
> to Rhône varieties.
>
> His years tasting the great wines of Europe shaped a simple philosophy: balance over power, and place over technique.
> That conviction is the foundation of Santi Rosina. In Happy Canyon, Doug makes Bordeaux and Italian varieties that are
> ripe enough to show California sunshine, yet restrained enough to honor the classics that inspired them.
>
> He has also consulted for Chêne Bleu in France, and has served on the Smithsonian's Kitchen Cabinet and the advisory
> board of the Julia Child Foundation.

Changed:
- *Wine Spectator* in italics (a publication's name).
- "Doug makes Bordeaux and Italian varieties that are ripe enough…" → "Doug makes wines from Bordeaux and Italian
  varieties: ripe enough…" (a winemaker makes wines, not varieties).
- "in France, and has served" → "in France and has served" (one subject, two verbs: no comma).

## Robert Daugherty — Head Winemaker

As received:

> Robert’s understanding and interest in winemaking developed at an early age while working alongside his aunt and
> uncle, Diana and Rick Longoria, owners and winemakers of Longoria Wines, one of Santa Barbara County’s legendary
> wineries. Under their mentorship, he learned the art and appreciation of crafting elegant, handcrafted and
> food-friendly wines. After graduating college, Robert lived in Europe, where he further honed his passion and
> appreciation for global wines. Robert then moved to New Zealand for two harvests where he learned larger scale
> winemaking practices. Upon returning to California, he spent four years with Dierberg / Star Lane on their cellar and
> viticulture team. After working with fruit from the appellations of Happy Canyon of Santa Barbara, Sta. Rita Hills and
> Santa Maria Valley, he then joined Winc as a start-up and helped significantly grow this business to its present size.
> Robert has been the head winemaker at Margerum Wine Company since 2023 where he’s overseen small batch winemaking.

As published (two paragraphs):

> Robert’s understanding of and interest in winemaking took root at an early age, when he worked alongside his aunt and
> uncle, Diana and Rick Longoria, the owners and winemakers of Longoria Wines, one of Santa Barbara County’s legendary
> wineries. Under their mentorship, he learned the art of making elegant, handcrafted, food-friendly wines. After
> graduating from college, Robert lived in Europe, where he deepened his passion for wines from around the world. He
> then spent two harvests in New Zealand, where he learned larger-scale winemaking.
>
> Back in California, he spent four years on the cellar and viticulture team at Dierberg and Star Lane, working with
> fruit from Happy Canyon of Santa Barbara, the Sta. Rita Hills and the Santa Maria Valley. He then joined Winc as a
> start-up and helped it grow significantly. Since 2023 Robert has been head winemaker at Margerum Wine Company, where he
> oversees small-batch winemaking.

Changed:
- "understanding and interest in" → "understanding of and interest in"; "developed … while working" said his
  understanding was working → "took root … when he worked".
- "the art and appreciation of crafting elegant, handcrafted and food-friendly wines" → "the art of making elegant,
  handcrafted, food-friendly wines" (crafting and handcrafted side by side).
- "After graduating college" → "After graduating from college"; "further honed his passion and appreciation for global
  wines" → "deepened his passion for wines from around the world" (a passion is not honed).
- "moved to New Zealand for two harvests where he learned larger scale winemaking practices" → "spent two harvests in
  New Zealand, where he learned larger-scale winemaking".
- The Dierberg and Winc sentences joined where they said the same thing twice ("After working with fruit from…"); the
  appellations given their articles (the Sta. Rita Hills, the Santa Maria Valley, as Doug's bio has it).
- **"to its present size" taken out**: Winc filed for bankruptcy in 2022 and was sold, so the phrase no longer reads true.
  Worth Remy's eye: say if the Winc line should go too.
- "has been the head winemaker … since 2023 where he’s overseen small batch winemaking" → "Since 2023 Robert has been
  head winemaker …, where he oversees small-batch winemaking".

> Viticulture and Agribusiness. Since then he has become one of Santa Barbara County's most respected viticulturists. He

> At Santi Rosina, Ben brings that same care to the vineyard in Happy Canyon. There, warm days and cool nights let

> estate's belief that great wine begins in the vineyard: balanced vines, careful timing and fruit that speaks clearly

Changed:
- The title takes the company's full name as his bio gives it, after a comma: "Co-Owner, Coastal Vineyard Care
  Associates".
- "a degree in Viticulture and Agribusiness" → "viticulture and agribusiness" (fields of study take no capitals).

## Juve Buenrostro — Vineyard Manager

As received:

> Juve brings more than two decades of experience in vineyard work and management in the Santa Ynez Valley. His deep
> knowledge of the valley's vineyards and his meticulous attention to detail make him an invaluable part of our team.
> Juve makes sure every grape reaches its fullest potential, capturing the unique terroir of the Santa Ynez Valley and
> helping to create exceptional wines.

Changed, by the copywriting guide:
- "meticulous" → "patient" (its *Word choices*: instead of meticulous, use patient).
- "capturing the unique terroir of the Santa Ynez Valley and helping to create exceptional wines" → "capturing the
  terroir of the Santa Ynez Valley in every bottle" (rule 1, "Don't tell the reader it's exceptional"; rule 2, one
  superlative per section: invaluable, fullest, unique and exceptional were four).
